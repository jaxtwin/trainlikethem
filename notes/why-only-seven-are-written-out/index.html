Why only seven of the thirty-five are written out in full

The other twenty-eight carry a summary and a source line. That gap is the whole editorial position, so it is worth saying plainly what causes it.

Every program on this site began as someone else's published claim: a press interview, a trainer's own post, a magazine feature, a documentary. Seven of them were published in enough detail that a reader can run the week — sets, reps, rest, and the food that went with it. Twenty-eight were not.

What counts as documented

A program gets written out in full when three things exist in public: a session-by-session structure, some statement of load or progression, and a calorie or macro figure that came from the person or their trainer rather than from a journalist's estimate. Miss any one of those and printing a full week would mean inventing the rest.

Most celebrity training coverage misses at least two. A feature will say "five days a week, heavy compounds, 4,000 calories" and stop. That is a summary, and we publish it as a summary.

The moment a single week is filled in with plausible guesses, no week on the site can be trusted, including the seven that are real.

What we will not do

The commercial temptation is obvious. A page titled "Henry Cavill's exact Superman workout" with a fabricated seven-day table will out-rank a page that admits the seven-day table was never published. We would rather lose that traffic. The moment a single week is filled in with plausible guesses, no week on the site can be trusted, including the seven that are real.

So the summary pages say what was reported, name where it was reported, and state what is missing. If the missing part later turns up — a trainer publishes the block, an interview goes into detail — the page gets upgraded and the change is noted.

The count will move

Seven of thirty-five is not a target. It is where the sourcing currently stands. It should climb, slowly, and only for the right reason.

One more thing worth being direct about: none of the people here are affiliated with this site, and nothing on it is endorsed by them. It is documentation of public claims, not a partnership.
