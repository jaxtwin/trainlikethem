What they all eat for breakfast

Strip the names off the seven documented first meals and six of them are the same three components. The interesting part is what varies and what doesn't.

22 Jul 20265 min readBuilt from 7 full write-ups

Of the thirty-five programs on this site, seven publish their food meal by meal. Their first plates of the day, as documented:

ZendayaGreek yogurt, granola, berries380

The same three components

Strip the names off and six of the seven are a protein source, a slow-digesting carbohydrate, and fruit or vegetables. Eggs or egg whites in four. Oatmeal in four. Berries or banana in three.

The range is wide — 380 calories to 1,100 — but the composition barely moves. What changes is portion size, and portion size is set by bodyweight and training load, not by any principle about breakfast.

Two things worth noticing

Two of the seven eat after training rather than before. Both are endurance athletes, and both run first. That's a scheduling decision, not a metabolic one: the session is the priority and the meal fits around it.

And the one plate that looks nothing like the others is the oldest. Arnold's whole eggs, steak and toast is a 1970s bodybuilding breakfast, and it's on the site because it was published, not because it's the recommendation. Every modern program here is leaner in the morning.

What it means for yours

There's no trick in these plates, which is the useful finding. Nobody in the documented set is eating anything unusual, expensive or difficult at breakfast. They're eating protein and oats.

If your morning meal has a protein source, a carbohydrate that isn't sugar, and something that grew, you're eating the same breakfast as seven documented programs. The rest is portion size against your own bodyweight.
