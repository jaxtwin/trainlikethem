Four supplements these programs name that you can skip

Twelve of the thirty-five programs list a multivitamin. That's a measure of what was popular when they were written, not of what works.

15 Jul 20262 min readBuilt from 35 documented programs

Every supplement named across the thirty-five programs is counted on the gear page, with a verdict against each one. Four of them we'd tell you not to bother with, and the counts explain why they're there at all.

Frequency isn't evidence

The multivitamin is the instructive case. Twelve of thirty-five programs name one, which makes it the second most common item in the whole set. If popularity in this sample were evidence, it would be the strongest recommendation on the site.

It isn't. It's a measure of how supplement stacks got written for two decades — a multivitamin was the default first line, the way a warm-up is the default first section. What twelve appearances tells you is what was conventional, not what was effective.

The dated ones

Glutamine and desiccated liver tablets are on the site for the same reason a museum keeps things: they were in the published program. Liver tablets appear once, in a routine from 1977, and we print them because printing only the parts that aged well would be a different kind of dishonesty.

BCAAs are the most recent of the four and the easiest to dismiss on arithmetic. If you're already hitting protein, you're already getting them. Buying them separately is buying a subset of something you own.

What we'd keep

Whey protein appears in six of the seven detailed stacks and creatine monohydrate in five. Those are the two items in the entire set with both frequency and evidence behind them, and between them they cost very little.

Electrolytes, magnesium and vitamin D3 are marked situational — worth testing on yourself for specific reasons, not worth taking by default.

The disclosure

Some links on the gear page are affiliate links, and the verdicts are not affected by them — four of the nine assessed items are marked skip, including the second most common supplement in the whole set. The full statement is on the disclosure page.
